Privacy, in plain words

Last checked against the code and the database on November 27, 2026.

The short version

  • What is stored: your email address and your saved game (what you tap or type).
  • Where: in a database at Supabase, the company that hosts the accounts of the game, and in a copy in this browser.
  • Who sees it: you, your coach only while "Share with my coach" is on, and Eli, the admin.
  • Delete: you can delete your account on this page, at Delete my account.
  • Your information is not sold, rented or traded. Not to anyone.
  • No affiliates. SLAM gets no money when you pick a card, a bank or any product that the game names.

The rest of this page says the same with all the details.

Who can see your game

  • You.
  • The person who runs SLAM (Eli, the admin). Eli can open any game and change it, for safety and support. This does not depend on any switch.
  • A coach, only while "Share with my coach" is on. While it is on, each account that Eli gave the coach role can open your game and change it.

How the switch starts. A new account from a public link starts with "Share with my coach" off. A new account from a coach's invite link starts with it on, and the sign-up screen says so. An account that already has a saved game keeps what it has.

Where the switch is. In the game: Menu, then My account, then Share with my coach. You can change it at any time.

When a coach or the admin opens your game to work in it, the game writes a line in a log that only the admin can read, and your Home screen tells you when they changed something from inside your game. A list that shows your name and your numbers to a coach or to the admin is not logged line by line.

SLAM the Game: what is stored, and where

In the database (Supabase, the company that hosts the accounts of the game):

  • Your email address, your password in a scrambled form that nobody can read, and the times you signed up and last signed in.
  • Your saved game: everything you tap or type in the game. The names of the people in it, income, spending, debts, savings, goals, your answers and your progress. It is saved as one game, cut in pieces.
  • Whether "Share with my coach" is on.
  • When you finish setup: the time, a share code made from your account (it is not your email), the share code of the link you arrived through, and your Founding Tester number if you are one of the first 50. You can read your own line. The admin sees who joined through whom.
  • In a guild (once Eli turns on guilds between accounts): the guild's name and day, who is in it, and only the things you switched on for the others to see (never a money number). Today a guild lives only inside one game on one device.
  • If you tap Report: what you chose (bug, idea or confusing), your note, the name of the screen and of the last 3 buttons you tapped, your device, browser and screen width, the app version, and a picture if you add one. No money number. You can read your own reports. The admin reads all of them. A report waits on your device until the database can take it.
  • If a save does not go through: one line with the time, your account and the technical reason, so the admin can repair it.
  • If you work with a coach: the notes and tasks your coach writes about your sessions. They are the coach's notes. You cannot read them in the app. The admin can.
  • If the admin turned on extra content for your account: which content, and when.
  • The admin log: who opened or changed which account, and when.

The admin can download one backup file with all accounts and games.

The weekly backup. When Eli switches it on, a copy of the database is made one time each week. The copy is locked with a passphrase that only Eli has. It is kept for 90 days at GitHub, the company that hosts the code of SLAM, and then GitHub removes it. A copy made before you deleted your account can hold your information until that copy is 90 days old.

On your device: a copy of your game in the storage of this browser, so the game opens fast and works with a weak connection. It also keeps you signed in.

Email: the email to confirm your address or to set a new password goes through an email service, which gets your email address for that.

Live sessions with a coach (Live Session Mode)

This part is built but not switched on yet. This is how it works when it is on.

In a live session your coach talks with you on a video call, and the session page of SLAM turns what each of you says into text, so your profile fills in while you talk. Both of you tap OK on the screen before anything starts.

  • Text only. SLAM records no audio and saves no audio. SLAM gets words, never sound.
  • Your browser turns the sound into words. In Chrome, this speech recognition sends the sound of your microphone to Google while you talk, and Google sends back the words. That is how the browser's speech recognition works; SLAM cannot turn it off. SLAM does not choose or see what Google keeps.
  • The words are saved in the database (Supabase), one line at a time, with which side said it and when. While the call runs they also travel between the two devices over Supabase's live link, which keeps nothing. The numbers heard, with the short quote each came from, are saved in your game.
  • After the call, AI may read the words. Only when your coach taps "Run the AI pass" (and only when Eli has switched that step on), the saved words of that call are sent to Anthropic, the company that makes the AI Claude, through a small program on Supabase. If your coach adds the text file of a recording of the call, that text goes too. The AI proposes numbers, and your coach checks each one before it goes in your profile. Anthropic's own terms cover what it receives.
  • Who can see the words of a session: you, the coach of that session, and the admin (Eli). Not other coaches. The short quotes kept with your numbers are in your game, so whoever can see your game (see "Who can see your game" above) can see those quotes.
  • Your coach's notes from a session, also the ones the AI pass drafts, are the coach's notes (see above). You cannot read them in the app.
  • How long the words are kept: until someone deletes them. After the call, you or your coach can tap "Delete this transcript": it removes the lines of that session from the database and the quotes from your game. The numbers stay, with who approved them and when, and the line "Transcript deleted on" the date. Delete my account removes them too, with the database step that also removes the sign-in (see Delete my account below).
  • If your coach recorded the call on a phone, that recording is on your coach's phone, not in SLAM. The app reminds your coach to delete it after the AI pass.

The SLAM web app (the planner and the short tools)

  • Your answers stay in the storage of your browser, on your device. The web app has no accounts on a server today, and it sends your numbers nowhere.
  • If you make a link to share, your answers travel inside that link. Whoever has the link can read them.
  • Files you save or export (a backup, a spreadsheet) are on your device, in your hands.

What SLAM does not do

  • No selling. Your information is not sold, rented or traded.
  • No affiliates. The links in the game carry no code that pays SLAM, and SLAM gets no money when you pick a card, a bank or a product.
  • No ads, and no tracking or analytics scripts.
  • No connection to your bank. SLAM never asks for a bank password.
  • Nothing in the code sends your information to any company other than the ones named on this page. In a live session that includes Google (the speech recognition of Chrome) and, only after the call when your coach runs the AI pass, Anthropic.

Like every website, the companies that deliver the pages see your internet address when the page loads: GitHub (the site), Supabase (the accounts), Google Fonts (the letter shapes), and jsDelivr and cdnjs (two code libraries).
